## Building a part

One part per session, on its own branch from `development`. A part session reads `README.md`, this file, and only the docs and code its part touches.

A part is done when:

1. It runs in Godot with no errors or warnings.
2. Everything in its Polish list is in: animated, with its sounds, nothing glossed over.
3. Claude has checked it by running the game, not just by reading the code.
4. Three screenshots of the finished part, taken from the running game, are saved in `screenshots/part_NN/` as `1.png`, `2.png` and `3.png`. Pick the three moments that show the part best.
5. Its Part notes are written. The branch is committed, pushed and merged into `development`, and `development` is pushed. Then check out `development`, so the next session starts there. The part session never waits for the user: it does the whole cycle, and the user reviews afterwards and asks for changes in a later session. This needs no git approval (see README, Handover 3).
6. The session disconnects from the Godot MCP, so the next one gets its port (see README, Handover 5).
7. The Godot editor is closed (after the MCP server is ended), because an open editor gets in the way of the overnight automation's screen watching. A Microplan session closes it too.
8. The session's last message ends with `q7Zk#x2` as a line of its own, which the overnight automation looks for (see README, Handover 6). A Microplan session ends the same way.

If a part turns out too big, split it (31a, 31b) instead of rushing it.

## Parts

Three parts: the last minigame, then the first two online parts. Head Soccer (37) plugs into `Minigames.GAMES` like the other three and reuses `CirclePhysics`. Parts 38 and 39 start the online play: a network layer over the relay from the spike, the lobby, and the board over the network. Everything online runs against a local `wrangler dev` relay and two or three Playwright tabs, so no part waits for the user's Cloudflare account; the real deploy moves to part 41 (see the Log).

**Shared by the minigames (as built in parts 33 to 36):**
- Every game is a `Minigame` subclass in `src/minigames/`, drawn in the frame's 1920 x 1080 space around `Minigame.TABLE_CENTER` (table 1480 x 640, y 220 to 860), reading keys only through `MinigameInput`, ending with `finish(side)` or `finish(Minigame.DRAW)`, and applying `edge` (Mr. Hyde: 0 or 0.1) invisibly. Registered in `Minigames.GAMES` (a `preload` for scripts). The challenger is side 0, on the left. Each lasts 20 to 30 seconds with no time limit. `step(delta)` is public for headless drivers.
- **Test habit:** a headless driver sets `MinigameInput.source` to a scripted function, steps the game with fixed time and asserts the winner; one short plain run per game for screenshots and FPS. A few real key presses through the MCP `simulate_key` prove the keys.
- **Keys:** left side W A S D and Q (action), E (special), R (dash); right side the arrows and K, L, ;. Keep each game to what one hand can do.

### Part 37: Head Soccer

**Goal:** a minigame space of kind `head_soccer` plays Head Soccer from the side: two big heads with little boots, a goal at each end, a bouncy pumpkin ball, first goal wins.

**Builds:**
- **`SoccerGame`** (`src/minigames/soccer_game.gd`), registered as `GAMES["head_soccer"]`. A side view on a drawn table (no table image): a night-sky strip and a ground line at the bottom of the 1480 x 640 table, `goal.png` at each end (mirrored on the left), `ball.png`, and each player a big portrait head (radius about 62) with two `boot.png` tinted by side under it. The challenger is on the left, defending the left goal.
- **Physics** in `_physics_process` with sub-steps (half a radius each), gravity for the ball and the heads, using `CirclePhysics` (`collide` for head to ball; add a small helper for the floor, walls, goal posts and crossbar, noted). Left and right (`direction(side).x`) run the head along the ground. **Jump** and **kick** use the second and third keys the frame already has (`just_pressed(side, "special")` is S's slot in the design; the design's S and D map to whichever of `special` and `dash` the frame's key table gives, and `controls()` shows the real keycaps). A jump is allowed on the ground only. A kick is a short boot swing (0.15 s, cooldown 0.4 s): if the ball is within reach it gets an impulse away from the foot, harder than a head bump. The ball does not speed up over time (Q17). Each head is clamped to its own half plus 80 px, so nobody camps in the other goal.
- **Goal:** the ball's centre past a goal line under the crossbar; the other side wins. Never a draw. If the ball rests on the ground for 5 seconds it is re-served with a hop from the middle, so a stalemate cannot happen (noted).
- **Hyde's edge:** the challenger's head runs 10% faster, invisible.
- **Sounds:** `kick` on a kick that touches the ball, `bounce` on the ground, posts and heads (pitch following the speed), `jump` on a jump. A landing is a hop with no sound (assets.md).

**Polish:**
- Heads squash on landing and on a hit, stretch on a jump and tilt into the run; the boots swing with the run and fling on a kick. Each head's shadow shrinks as it rises.
- The ball spins with its horizontal speed and squashes along the line of a hit; a puff of paper bits where it touches a head or a boot.
- On a goal the net jiggles once and puffs paper bits, the scoring head hops and the other droops, before the frame's win beat.
- The ball is held up for 0.4 s at the start and then dropped, so neither side gets a free first touch.
- `controls()` gives the keycap lines: run left/right, jump, kick (`KEYS_SOCCER`, a new string, Turkish too).

**Assets:** `goal`, `ball`, `boot`, `kick`, `jump`, `bounce`, the portraits, and the board's sky for the backdrop. None new. Check which way `goal.png` faces (the notes say right).

**Check:**
- Headless: the ball falls, bounces and settles without tunnelling at kick speed; a jump lands back; a standing head loses to a scripted shooter; a kick sends the ball away from the foot; a goal ends it with the right winner; the stall re-serve fires; Hyde's head is faster; the half clamp holds.
- A plain run through the frame with a forced Head Soccer space and scripted input, 25 FPS, then a few real keys through the MCP.
- Screenshots: the opening drop, a kick mid-swing, the goal.

**Risks:** the feel (ball bounciness, gravity, jump height, kick reach) decides the game, so tune by playing, then again in part 42. Two hands on one keyboard (run, jump, kick) may ghost: keep to one key held per hand where possible.

### Part 38: Online I, the network layer and the lobby

**Goal:** from the title, Play lets one person host a room (a 4-letter code and a Copy link) and others join by code or link, type a name, click Ready, and the host starts; everyone lands on the same turn-order screen. Local mode is unchanged.

**Builds:**
- **`Net`** (autoload, `src/net/net.gd`): one `WebSocketPeer` to the relay (`/room/<CODE>`), the relay URL a constant overridable by `?relay=` or `-- --relay`, polled in `_process`, our own JSON (`{to, data}` out; `hello|join|leave|msg` in, as in the spike). Signals `peer_joined(id)`, `peer_left(id)`, `received(from, kind, payload)`. A tiny RPC layer: `Net.send(to, kind, payload)` and `Net.broadcast(kind, payload)`. `Net.is_host`, `Net.my_id`. It reconnects once on a drop (the full rejoin is part 41). No timer may depend on a background tab's answers (spike finding).
- **`tools/relay/`** kept and tidied from the spike; the throwaway `src/spike/` and the `--spike` hook in `title.gd` are deleted. The relay learns two things: the first socket into a fresh room is told it is the host (`hello` carries `host: true`), and a ninth is refused with a `full` message. Tested against `wrangler dev`.
- **Lobby scene** (`src/lobby/`): Play on the title opens it: Host room / Join room (a code field), then the room view: the code in big letters, Copy link (`JavaScriptBridge` on the web, `DisplayServer.clipboard_set` on desktop), seat cards (name, ready tick, host crown), a name field, Ready, and Start (host only, enabled with 3 to 8 ready players). The link is `<page url>?room=CODE` and goes straight to the name prompt. The host keeps the lobby state and broadcasts it on every change.
- **The hand-off:** Start sends the player list and a seed to everyone; each copy creates the same `Match` through the normal setup path (the seed fills `Match.board_rng` and the turn order, so all copies roll the same order and board) and goes to the turn order roll.

**Polish:**
- Seat cards slide in with a hop and the `join` sound and drop out with `leave`; the Ready tick pops with `ready`; the code letters sit on cut-paper tiles; Start plays `start` and the curtain closes.
- Copy link flashes "Copied!" on the button. A full room, a missing room, a dropped connection and a taken name each show a short cut-paper banner (new strings, Turkish too).
- Names are trimmed and limited to 12 characters; an empty name becomes "Player N". The code alphabet avoids lookalikes (no I, O, 0, 1).

**Assets:** `join`, `leave`, `ready`, `start` (all exist), the title's curtain and panel images. None new.

**Check:**
- Headless: the JSON encode and decode, the seat rules (3 to 8 to start, the ninth refused, trimming, duplicate names), the code alphabet.
- Playwright: three tabs against `wrangler dev` (a host and two joiners, one by link), ready, start; all three land on the turn order roll with the same names and order. `bringToFront` before typing.
- Local mode still starts a match.
- Screenshots: the room with three seats, a seat joining, the full-room banner.

**Risks:** the web export has to be rebuilt for the Playwright check (13 s through `build_release.py`). `JavaScriptBridge` is web-only: guard it. If it is too big, split it: 38a the `Net` layer and relay change with a plain text lobby, 38b the real lobby scene.

### Part 39: Online II, the board over the network

**Goal:** a match started from the lobby plays on every tab: the host's copy runs the rules, everyone sees the same board, and each player decides only for their own piece, with the decision timers running.

**Builds:**
- **Authority:** the host's `Match` is the truth. Every decision (roll the die, pick a path, pick an opponent and a stake or walk away, pick a monster) is a request: a player's copy shows the choice UI only on their turn and sends `decision` to the host, which validates it against the rules and applies it. The host's `Board._play_turns` and the monster-select loop are the only drivers; the other copies replay what the host broadcasts.
- **Events, not state sync:** the host broadcasts an ordered stream of events (`turn_started`, `rolled`, `moved`, `landed`, `blood_changed`, `power`, `challenge_chosen`, and so on) carrying the values the rules used, and every copy plays the same animation from them. The board and HUD already animate from `Match` changes, so the work is routing those calls through one `Events` seam (like `MinigameInput`): local mode calls straight through, online mode sends and receives. Read `Board._play_turns`, `land` and the power pops first, and write the event list in the Part notes.
- **Timers:** the decision timers from `game_rules.md` (die 5 s, path 10 s, challenge 20 s, monster 30 s) are real countdowns on the host. When one ends, the host makes the random valid choice and broadcasts it as if the player had made it. A gone or background player plays by the same timers.
- **Views:** only the active player gets the clickable die and paths; others see "Name is rolling...". The camera follows the same piece on every copy.
- **Minigame spaces for now:** until part 40, online minigame spaces are settled by the host with a random winner and a short "Fate decides" banner (noted), so a match can be played to the end.

**Polish:**
- The countdown is a small cut-paper ring on the active card that shrinks, with a `ui_click` tick at 3, 2, 1 and a flash at zero. An automatic choice pops "Auto", so nobody wonders who chose.
- Dice and moves play the same on every copy, a hair after the active one, so the result lands together.
- "Your turn" slides in on the active player's own tab with the `ready` sound; the HUD card of the active player glows as it already does.

**Assets:** none new.

**Check:**
- Headless: the event list round-trips through JSON; the host rejects a decision from the wrong player or out of turn; each timer picks a valid random choice; two copies fed the same event stream end with the same `Match` state (a hash compare).
- Playwright: three tabs play a whole short 3-player match, one clicking only on its turns, one never clicking (the timers play it), and all three show the same trophies. Shorten the turns with a debug flag for the test.
- Screenshots: the active player's view, a waiting player's view, a timer ring.

**Risks:** the biggest online part, since the events seam has to be found by reading the board code. Split it if needed: 39a the seam and the board turn, 39b the timers, monster select and the end screens. Copies never recompute the rules from inputs; the host sends results, so float differences cannot matter.
